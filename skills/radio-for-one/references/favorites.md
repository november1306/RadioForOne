# Favorites — read a list into the dossier

A favorites list shows the pattern; the interview asks what it can't. Your job here
is to turn a list into **hypotheses and questions**, never into confident claims.

## Steps

1. **Take any form**: a playlist export, `title | uploader` lines, artist names,
   prose, a screenshot. For a YouTube playlist URL with a shell available:
   `yt-dlp --flat-playlist --print "%(title)s | %(uploader)s" <url>`. Store the
   input verbatim under Raw listening data with date and source.
2. **Normalize artists.** An uploader is often not the artist: `- Topic` channels
   are the artist, fan and label channels are not. Fix obvious ones; mark the
   unsure.
3. **Ask what the list is**: "Is all of this stuff you love, or partly saved
   history?" An endorsed list makes every entry a like; a history list needs
   weighting.
4. **Map it.**
   - **Families and functions**: cluster tracks (guitar band, electronic,
     piano/instrumental, score, hip-hop, theatrical, national scenes) and guess
     what each cluster is *for* (energy, background, attentive, comfort). Give
     rough shares — they set the curator's spread and energy targets.
   - **Colors**: a trait present in every cluster (melancholy, aggression, humor)
     is a color, not a genre.
   - **Center**: the scene and era that look formative; check it in the interview.
   - **Satellites and outliers**: zones the center can't explain, and single tracks
     that fit nothing.
   - **Leans** (a country, a label, an era): a lean is real only against a base
     rate — compare with how common that origin is overall before calling it.
5. **Write the dossier.** The list goes into Favorites / Raw listening data; every
   inference enters the profile as `low` or `med`, status `untested`; every
   track or cluster the profile can't explain becomes an Open question ("You have
   eleven downtempo tracks — what are they for?").

Done when every cluster has a hypothesized function, the center has a candidate,
and the unexplained parts are listed as questions for the interview.
